# U.S. E-Commerce Trends
*TBD once the Nov 19 release is available. It is delayed for unknown reasons. Below is the previous text.*

The e-commerce share of U.S. retail is growing slightly. Growth in revenue is 2-3% above inflation, far below the heydays of 10-40% annual growth.

Our view is that e-commerce is approaching its equilibrium level. This equilibrium looks to be below 20-25% of total retail. However, new data with higher growth will change this estimate.

This points to the need for e-commerce players, especially Amazon, to re-jig their strategies. The economics of e-commerce still favor them, but there is a need to find better value propositions for consumers. 

Further, he vast majority of household spending is not in consumer goods, but in education, health care, and housing. Adjacent products or services in these areas should be explored. 

Further expansion in consumer goods seems unlikely to yield major pay-off since everything has been covered already. This may change if virtual reality ever happens.

Further, the tariffs will lead to a negative impact for all retail. With a price elasticity of −1, we can expect retail sales to fall 5-8% even if the new tariffs end up at a 10% effective rate (this assumes producers and wholesalers absorb 20-50% of the tariff impact, otherwise the fall will be around 10%).
